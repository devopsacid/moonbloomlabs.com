## L · LinkedIn founder · Launch story

Today I'm launching Moonbloom Labs.

The idea is simple: you describe an idea in the evening, and the next morning you can try it.

Here's how a night works:
Evening: we talk for a short while and cut the idea to a scope that fits one night.
Night: AI agents write the code and its tests, following a plan I've reviewed.
Morning: you get a working prototype, its tests, a short demo and a list of open decisions.
Decision: approve it, refine it over another night, or plan the path to production.

What it isn't: a finished product in one night. I'll always tell you in the evening what fits, and in the morning what is still missing.

What it is: the fastest honest way I know to find out whether an idea works.

We build RAG search over your documents, knowledge bases, AI assistants, gamification, AI integrations, and the DevOps and cloud work to take a good prototype to production.

Have an idea for tonight? https://moonbloomlabs.com

<a id="one-night"></a>

## L+8 · LinkedIn founder · What fits into one night

"Overnight" sounds like a stretch, so here's what actually fits into one night:

One clear idea with a core flow you can try. A chat over a sample of your documents. An assistant for one support topic. A connection between two systems.

What doesn't fit: a whole product. If your idea is bigger, we build the part that answers your most important question first, and plan the rest as further nights.

The scope conversation in the evening is the most important half hour of the whole process.

## L+15 · LinkedIn founder · A human decides

Image: quote card "A human decides. Agents build."

People ask who checks the work when agents build overnight. The answer is: people, at both ends.

In the evening, you approve the scope and I review the plan. In the morning, you review the prototype, its tests and a list of open decisions, the choices agents should not make for you. Nothing ships without human approval.

Agents are very good at building. Deciding what's worth building is still our job.

<a id="production"></a>

## L+27 · LinkedIn founder · From prototype to production

A prototype that works in the morning is the start, not the end.

When you approve one, the work changes: CI/CD, infrastructure as code, monitoring, hardening, access rights. That's the DevOps and cloud side of Moonbloom, and it's where I've spent most of my 20+ years.

Prototypes come with tests and a clear structure for exactly this reason: so the good ones can grow up.
